Call Scripts

Openers, gatekeeper paths, voicemails, booking language, and discovery — ready to use.

Cold open · pattern interrupt
"Hey, it's [your name] — you weren't expecting my call, so I'll be quick. I work with distribution ops leaders on [specific pain from intel]. Did I catch you at a terrible time, or just a bad one?"
Cold open · referral/trigger
"Hi [name], I came across the announcement about your new facility in [location] — congrats on the expansion. I work with DC leaders who are in that exact build-out phase and I had one idea worth 60 seconds. Is now okay?"
Gatekeeper path
"I promised [exec]'s team a benchmark before Friday — could you point me to whoever owns her calendar?"

If pushed: "I'm following up on a conversation about the new facility timeline. I just need 20 minutes — who's the best person to schedule through?"
Voicemail · 18 seconds
"[Name], one idea about the new facility and a number worth hearing: 9,000 hours. I'll try you Thursday at 10 — or grab me sooner at [your number]."
Booking the meeting
"Sounds like there's something here. I'm not asking you to evaluate anything — 20 minutes with our solutions consultant to pressure-test the math on your operation. Tuesday or Thursday better?"
Discovery questions
• What does a mis-ship cost you today — in dollars and in customer relationships?
• Who feels peak season first — ops, IT, or finance?
• If the new facility slips a quarter, what breaks?
• What did the last systems project teach the team about what to avoid?
• Walk me through a typical order from receipt to ship — where does it slow down?
• What's your current visibility across sites look like?
Executive intro (NAED / trade show follow-up)
"Great meeting you at [event]. You mentioned [specific thing they said] — that's exactly the problem we solve. I'd like to get 20 minutes with you and your DC team to show you what that looks like in practice. What does your calendar look like next week?"

Objection Handling

The counters that actually work — organized by objection.

"We just did a big systems project / ERP migration"
Validate, then reframe: "That's exactly why this works — WMS rides on top of what you just built. The question is what the DC team gave up in the migration. Most ERP implementations deprioritize warehouse execution to hit the go-live date. Is that what happened here?"
"We're happy with our current system"
"Most of our best customers said the same thing — until they saw what 'happy' was costing per order. We've got a customer in your exact vertical who thought the same thing and found $2M in operational waste in a 90-day assessment. Worth a benchmark just to know your number?"
"No budget this year"
"Totally fair. What's the manual workaround costing in the meantime? If the math says the do-nothing option is the expensive one, the budget conversation changes. I'm not asking for a commitment — just the numbers to make that case internally if it makes sense."
"Send me some information"
"Happy to — but generic decks waste your time. Give me 90 seconds on what your pick process looks like and I'll send the one page that's actually relevant. What's your biggest throughput challenge right now?"
"We're already looking at [competitor]"
"Good — you should be evaluating options. One thing worth knowing: Infor WMS has been a Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader 8 consecutive years. That's not marketing — it means analysts have validated our execution every year. What criteria are you using to evaluate? I want to make sure you're comparing the right things."
"We're not ready / timing isn't right"
"Understood. The customers who are happiest with their go-lives are the ones who started the conversation 6 months before they were 'ready.' What would need to be true for timing to be right? I'd rather be your first call when that changes than your fourth."
"IT is the bottleneck / IT owns this decision"
"Totally makes sense — and IT is a critical stakeholder. In our experience though, the business case usually starts with ops. If the DC team can show IT a clear ROI and a low-disruption integration path, the conversation moves faster. Can we build that case together before we loop in IT?"

Email Templates

Proven frames for cold touch, follow-up, break-up, and re-engagement. Edit — don't start from blank.

Cold · trigger-based

New facility / expansion

Saw the announcement — congrats on the expansion.

Most teams discover the systems gap 90 days before go-live, when it's expensive to fix. One customer in your space solved it early and banked a 40% productivity increase and 9,000 hours saved annually.

Worth 15 minutes to see if the math works for your operation?

— Brian

Cold · leadership change

New exec / VP started

Subject: congrats on the new role

Congrats on the new role at [company].

New supply chain leaders usually inherit a short list of things they'd change if they could — warehouse execution is usually on it.

I work with DC ops teams on exactly that. Would a quick 20-minute call be worth your time in the next few weeks?

— Brian

Follow-up

After no response (touch 2–3)

Subject: RE: [original subject]

Bumping this up — still think the timing is relevant given [specific trigger].

If this isn't a priority right now, totally fine to say so. Otherwise, 20 minutes is all I need.

— Brian

Break-up

Last touch

Subject: closing the loop

Haven't heard back so I'll assume timing's off. No hard feelings — these decisions have long cycles.

One ask: when warehouse systems come up at planning time, keep this thread. The benchmark offer stands.

— Brian

Re-engagement

Trigger event re-open

Subject: [trigger] — circling back

Saw [acquisition/new hire/facility announcement] and thought of our conversation from [timeframe].

Timing has a way of changing. Worth a quick call to see if the picture looks different now?

— Brian

Robotics

Robotics Integration

Native integration with major robotics and automation vendors — not a bolt-on. Use when automation or robotics is already on the prospect's roadmap or they've visited a competitor demo with robotics.

Positioning

"Rides on top of your ERP"

WMS is not a replacement for their ERP — it's a layer on top that handles what ERP was never designed for: real-time warehouse execution, labor management, and multi-site visibility. Use when ERP fatigue or recent ERP migrations are the objection.

Competitive

Total Cost of Ownership

The "do nothing" option has a cost too — mis-ships, overtime, manual workarounds, and missed SLAs. Use to reframe budget objections: "The question isn't what this costs — it's what not doing it is costing you right now."
